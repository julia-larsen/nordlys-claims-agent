# Experiments

Format: run_id | model | prompt | max_steps | dev accuracy | cost/claim (p50) | what changed / notes

- `smoke1` | small | v1 | 12 | n/a (1 claim) | — | First real API call. `search_handbook`'s excerpt was a naive first-220-chars slice; for S1 that cut off before the tier→city table. Model thrashed on repeated `search_handbook` calls for 12 steps and never converged. Bug, not model failure — excerpt now centers on the earliest matched keyword instead of always starting at char 0, and single-letter/stopword tokens (`a`, `the`, ...) are filtered out of the tokenizer so they can't anchor the excerpt or pad the overlap score.
- `smoke2` | small | v1 | 12 | 1/1 correct decision | €0.0065 | Same claim after the `search_handbook` fix: converged in 5 steps. Amount off by €0.05 (845.98 vs 845.93, rounding). Citation recall 1.0, exact-match 0.0 — label's `applied_rules` only lists sections that changed the outcome, agent cites everything it consulted. Decided to score citations both ways (recall vs exact) rather than pick one.
- `dev_small_v1` | small | v1 | 12 | 0.600 | €0.00909 | Full 20-claim dev baseline. All reject cases (S10, late submission) misclassified as approve; escalate recall only 0.25; 3/20 hit the step ceiling.
- `dev_small_v2` | small | v2 | 12 | 0.684 (n=19, one claim crashed with a transient litellm error and — bug — wasn't logged as an episode, so it silently dropped out of scoring) | €0.01093 | Added a 4-question claim-level checklist to the system prompt (FX/deadline/budget/pre-approval) before line-item work, based on reading full trajectories: the model retrieved S10 in `dev_small_v1` and still approved a claim submitted 126 days late — it read the rule but never did the day-count arithmetic. Also fixed the crash-swallowing bug so failed episodes always get an `episode_end` record.
- `dev_small_v3` | small | v2 | 12 | 0.600 (n=20) | €0.01055 | Re-run after the crash-logging fix. Reject recall improved 0 → 0.33 and escalate recall 0.25 → 0.5, but step-limit hits rose to 4/20 (checklist adds steps) and approve recall dropped, netting to the same overall accuracy. Read a step-limited trajectory (C-001) — genuine multi-step complexity (5 line items, no repeated calls), not thrashing.
- `dev_small_v4` | small | v2 | **16** | 0.650 | €0.01416 | Raised `MAX_STEPS` 12→16 on the evidence above. Step-limit rate stayed at 0.15 (different claims). Confusion matrix given a `no_decision` column so per-class rows always sum to the true count — previously step-limited claims counted in recall denominators but vanished from the matrix. **Headline finding, held across all 4 runs: every true-reject case gets approved instead** (0/3 reject recall this run). Read C-017: model asserted "submitted within 90 days" when the actual gap is 102 days — confident, wrong, and unshown arithmetic, despite an explicit instruction to compute the exact day count. Stopping small-model prompt iteration here per the challenge's own overfitting warning; this is the reported failure mode, not something to keep patching against 20 dev claims.
- `dev_large_v2` | large | v2 | 16 | 0.800 (accuracy given decided: 1.000, n=16/20) | €0.0767 | Task 5 cost/quality comparison, same prompt and step ceiling as `dev_small_v4`. Every large-model error on this run is a `no_decision` (step limit) — zero wrong decisions among claims it actually resolved. Reject/escalate recall both 1.0, vs 0/0.5 for small. ~5.4x cost/claim of small. Added `accuracy_given_decided` and a `no_decision` confusion-matrix column to `src/eval.py` to make this visible (previously step-limited claims silently vanished from the matrix while still counting against per-class recall).
- `holdout_large_final` | large | v2 | 16 | **0.825 (accuracy given decided: 1.000, n=33/40) — FINAL, scored once** | €0.0618 | Chosen configuration (large model + treat `no_decision` as auto-route-to-human) scored on the 40-claim holdout after the dev-set decision was locked in. Confirms the dev-set finding at higher n: reject/escalate recall both 1.0, zero wrong decisions among resolved claims. Higher tool-error rate (0.15 vs 0.05) fully explained by 5 claims hitting the designed missing-FX-rate case — all 5 escalated correctly. Total cost €2.73 for 40 claims.
